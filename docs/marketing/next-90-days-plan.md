## 3. Go-to-market review vs vision

The GTM review (Jul) is a rep playbook for schools. The vision (keynote §5, Oct) says why Drone Edge exists: close the gap between drone technology and communities, through schools, by backing the teacher. Both agree schools are the mission. Neither says how to make money in the next 90 days.

| Topic | GTM review (Jul) | Vision (Oct) | What it means for the next 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary customer** | Schools, rep-led; B2C "secondary" | Schools as the distribution channel; the teacher is the bottleneck | Schools are the mission. **Consumers are where the cash and the first proof come from now.** |
| **What we sell** | Part 107 live + building "standing up" | Device + content + teacher support + showcase | Only **Part 107** is sellable before January. Building is the reason to stay on the list. |
| **Proof** | None yet; case study after the first pilot | "Measure from day one, claim nothing early" | **Paying consumers produce the first honest proof** (reviews, "what helped me" quotes). That unblocks paid tiers 3–4 for both segments. |
| **Awareness** | SEO articles, school outreach | Awareness is the deepest gap; lateral (peer-to-peer) channels spread fastest | **Social and communities are the lateral channel.** Make content people share, not press releases. |
| **The teacher** | Manager dashboard as a feature | Teacher support is the product to win on (2b) | **Teacher-as-learner:** get teachers certified as individuals first. That's the vision's teacher readiness, sold one person at a time. |
| **Segments** | CTE buyers + career changers | Hobbyists as the "some already know" early adopters | **Builders/makers are missing from the GTM review.** The waitlist and Bench notes keep them warm until the build course ships. |
| **Content mix** | School articles + careers | Practical, useful without the sale | Most live articles are geopolitics and defense news. They bring readers who don't buy a Part 107 course. **Shift output to consumer how-to content.** |
| **Staleness** | Says $129 "after deploy", no newsletter, rep not started | — | Refresh the GTM review after the Dec 15 review; this doc covers the gap until then. |

**Takeaway:** the vision's playbook (back the teacher, build public awareness, let it spread peer to peer) works for consumers too. For the next 90 days, run it with individuals: consumers and teachers buying for themselves, reached through faceless social, communities and the newsletter. Then take the proof and the teachers into school sales in spring.

## 11. Reference teacher: what to collect and what to make (Oct 6 – Nov 6)

### 11.1 Before the call

- Pull her class's **manager dashboard** numbers for your own prep: students enrolled, active seats (30 days), units completed, unit exam participation and averages. These are **internal** until she and the district say they can be shared.
- **Disclosure (decided Oct 6):** her school **paid for seats at a heavy pilot discount**, and she has a **prior relationship** with our team. Both are material connections under the FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255.5), even though she'll be honest. Every public use of her quote carries a short line, e.g. *"Her school bought seats at a discounted pilot rate; she knew our team before the pilot."* (no founder name; see the brand-anonymity rule). One line under the quote is enough; don't bury it.
- **Never publish or quote her discounted price.** It isn't a reference price for other schools.
- **The grant is unrelated** to the seats or the discount. Naming the school in a quote needs only her and the district's OK. Naming the grant-funded drone project (e.g. at the Boston talk) still needs the grant's publicity clause checked (keynote doc V2).
- **Friendly-source bias:** she'll be honest, but she's also kind to us. Weight her complaints more than her praise, and ask for specifics ("which lesson", "which week") rather than "how's it going".

### 11.2 The call (~30 min): what to find out

Ask open questions and write down her **exact words**. Quotes have to be hers, not paraphrased.

| Topic | Questions | Feeds |
|---|---|---|
| **Context** | Course and pathway name, grade levels, students per section, period length and periods per week, devices (Chromebooks?), when they started, which drones they fly | Case study, pacing guide, one-pager |
| **How she runs it** | Teacher-led, hybrid or self-paced? What she does in class around the lessons (flying days, discussion, worksheets)? Weeks per unit? Where are they now? | **Pacing guide** draft, `/schools/curriculum` |
| **What works** | Best part for her? For students? A moment students got into it? *Why* do they like it as much as or more than regular classwork? | Quotes, ad hooks, Field Notes Classroom corner |
| **What doesn't** | Confusing lessons, blurry images, missing videos, dashboard gaps, sign-up and invite friction (TC1), anything that wasted a period | Product backlog → `TODO.md` |
| **Her own prep** | Has she taken Part 107 herself? Does she want to? What training would have helped? | Teacher-as-learner campaign, [`../sales/teacher-training.md`](../sales/teacher-training.md) |
| **Outcomes** | Any students planning to take the FAA test, and when? (School-reported only; never a pass-rate claim) | Case study (later) |
| **Buying** | Who paid or would pay, and how (grant, CTE budget, Perkins)? Is there budget for spring or next year? Who signs off? More sections next year? Interest in the build course and kits (2 Neos vs the 4 she wanted)? | **Spring quote = income**, kit demand |
| **Her network** | Other teachers she'd tell? Which conferences, Facebook groups or lists do PA CTE teachers actually use? | Referrals; **real data for the expo decision** |
| **Permission** | See 11.3 | Everything public |

### 12.2 What we get in every case

These are worth the call even in the worst case:

1. **Product fix list:** real classroom feedback, which no ad test can give us.
2. **Pacing data:** how a real class moves through the units, for the pacing guide and `/schools/curriculum`.
3. **How schools actually buy:** who signs, which budget, when. This applies to every school we approach in spring.
4. **A private reference.** Even if nothing can go public, ask: "Would you take one 10-minute call from a teacher considering us?" Private references need no district approval, and for B2B they often beat a public quote.
5. **Where PA teachers gather** (conferences, groups), for the expo decision.

## 13. Sectional chart crops: is there a market?

**Not as a standalone paid product.** FAA sectionals are public domain and free, and free tools already show them (FAA's own charts, SkyVector, Aloft/B4UFLY for LAANC). Nobody pays for a crop. What has value is the **explanation**: "here's what's around Philadelphia for a drone pilot, and what each marking means." Our crop script ([`../../scripts/sectional_crop.py`](../../scripts/sectional_crop.py)) makes each metro cheap to produce, and several charts are already downloaded (New York, Washington, Charlotte, Atlanta, DFW, Seattle and others).

| Use | Who | Value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metro airspace articles** ("Where can you fly a drone in Philadelphia / DC / Boston?") with annotated crops | Consumers searching their city | Local "can I fly here" searches are real demand; DC (the FRZ/SFRA) is the classic question | **Do it: SEO + ad landing pages.** 1 metro per month, starting with Philadelphia and DC |
| **"Your school on the sectional"**: one personalized annotated crop of the area around a school | Teachers | Local relevance; a strong first-email or meeting hook at near-zero cost | **Do it: sales hook**, free in outreach and for paying schools |
| **Classroom chart pack** (printable local crops + worksheet) | Schools | Part of the program, not a separate sale | Include in school seats; maybe a kit add-on later |
| **Metro pack as a Pro benefit** | Pro subscribers | Gives Pro a recurring reason (money-model § 3.3) | Yes, once there are 3+ metros |
| **Paid standalone metro pack** ($9–19) | Consumers | Low willingness to pay; free alternatives | **No** |

Rules: every crop says **"For training only, not for navigation. Chart edition [date]"**, because sectionals change every 56 days. Be honest that the FAA exam uses figures from its own testing supplement; local charts are extra practice, not the test. Check which sectional covers each city before cropping.
