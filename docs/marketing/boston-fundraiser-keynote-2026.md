## 2. The one structural decision

Everything else follows from this. Pick a path first, then read §3.

### Path A — the charity solicits; Drone Edge speaks and donates ✅ **recommended**

Money flows **donor → charity's own payment page → charity's own bank account.** Drone Edge never receives, holds, or re-grants a dollar. Your role on stage is storyteller and donor, not collector.

- Charity of record, in preference order: **(1) the school district itself** — a governmental unit, so gifts for public purposes are deductible under IRC §170(c)(1) and there is no state charity-registration question; **(2) the district's education foundation**; **(3) the PTO**; **(4) a fiscal sponsor** (a regional community foundation) if none of the above can process cards in four weeks.
- Drone Edge takes **zero compensation** tied to the fundraiser. Under M.G.L. c. 68 §18, "professional solicitor" turns on *being paid to solicit* — unpaid speakers are outside it.
- No raffle, no auction, no ticket sales, no merch sales at the ask. This single choice collapses most of §3.
- Cost: you give up control of the money and depend on the school moving fast. Accept that.

### Path B — Drone Edge collects and re-grants ❌ **avoid**

Advertising that your for-profit's event presence benefits a charity puts you in **commercial co-venturer** territory in Massachusetts (written contract, filing, bonding, post-campaign accounting under M.G.L. c. 68 §§18–24 and 940 CMR 2.00). Four weeks is not enough time. It also makes you a fiduciary over restricted funds, muddies your books, and hands anyone who wants to make trouble the "the vendor raised money that came back to the vendor" story.

### Path C — in-kind only 🟡 **strong fallback**

Ask for **hardware, not cash**: "sponsor a drone," donors ship airframes/batteries directly to the district, board accepts them under its gift policy, district issues the acknowledgment. Zero money handling, zero solicitation-registration surface, fastest possible setup. **If A2 can't be answered by Oct 10, do this.** It also plays well in a hardware room.

Practical recommendation: **A with C running alongside.** Cash QR for the charity, plus a "we'll take airframes" line for the companies present.

### 2.1 Setting up the charity of record

**Decided (Sep 24):** no money goes to Drone Edge. Four options, fastest first. **For Oct 23, only 1 and 2 are realistic.**

| # | Option | Time | Cost | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **District accepts directly** | Days–weeks (board cycle) | $0 | **Best for Oct 23** |
| 2 | **Existing affiliated 501(c)(3)** — education foundation, or a PTO/booster that is *actually* tax-exempt | ~1–2 weeks | $0 + platform fees | Best if district can't take cards |
| 3 | **Fiscal sponsor** — e.g. a county community foundation | 2–6 weeks | 5–10% of gifts | Multi-school or multi-year program |
| 4 | **Form a new 501(c)(3)** | 1–6 months | ~$400–$900 + ongoing filings | Only if this becomes a standing program |

**1 — District direct.** PA school boards are authorized to accept gifts under the Public School Code, usually through a board gifts/grants/donations policy (PSBA template Policy 702). A district is a governmental unit, so a gift for public purposes is deductible under IRC §170(c)(1) with no 501(c)(3) needed; the district issues the written acknowledgment for gifts ≥ $250. Government units generally fall outside charity-registration regimes — confirm for MA. Ask the business manager:
- Can the board accept a gift **restricted to the UAS / drone program**, and which account does it sit in? (It must not land in the general fund.)
- Does the district's payment portal (the one used for fees, trips, lunch) support a **donation item** with a public link/QR?
- Which board meeting accepts it, and does it need pre-approval to *solicit* in the district's name?
- Who signs acknowledgment letters?
- Can it accept **in-kind hardware**, and how is it inventoried and insured?

Risk: districts move slowly and some can't take online card gifts at all. Checks + pledge cards + in-kind still work.

**2 — Existing 501(c)(3).** Ask whether the district has an education foundation, and whether the PTO/booster is actually exempt — many aren't, or have lapsed. Verify on the **IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search** (active status, no auto-revocation). The org opens a **restricted fund** ("UAS program"), signs a short MOU with the district on how funds become equipment, and runs the page on a nonprofit platform. A PA organization soliciting in Boston may technically trigger MA registration — confirm with the MA AG's division.

**3 — Fiscal sponsor.** A community foundation or a national sponsor holds a named fund. Model C ("pre-approved grant") is lighter than Model A ("comprehensive"). Expect an application, fees, and weeks of onboarding. Good if this expands to several districts.

**4 — Own 501(c)(3)** (PA nonprofit corporation):
1. Name check; file **Articles of Incorporation – Nonprofit** with PA Dept. of State (DSCB:15-5306). PA also requires **advertising the incorporation** in two newspapers, one of them a legal journal, in the county of the registered office.
2. EIN; bylaws; board (use 3+ independent directors); conflict-of-interest policy.
3. **IRS Form 1023-EZ** (~$275, only if projected receipts ≤ $50k/yr for 3 yrs and assets ≤ $250k; often weeks) or full **Form 1023** (~$600; months). Exemption is retroactive to incorporation if filed within 27 months.
4. **PA BCCO registration** before soliciting (exempt below $25k/yr contributions if no paid solicitor); **MA AG registration** before soliciting in MA.
5. Ongoing: Form 990-N/990-EZ/990, PA renewal, board minutes, acknowledgment letters.

## 3. Legal analysis

### 3.1 Charitable solicitation

| Jurisdiction | What bites |
|---|---|
| **Massachusetts** (M.G.L. c. 68 §§18–35; 940 CMR 2.00) | Charities soliciting in MA register with the AG's Public Charities Division. **Professional solicitors** (paid to solicit) must register, post a **$25,000 bond**, file the contract and a notice *before* the campaign, and report after. **Commercial co-venturers** (a business advertising that an activity benefits a charity) need a filed written contract and an accounting. Path A is designed to keep you out of both categories. |
| **MA raffles** (M.G.L. c. 271 §7A) | Permit from the clerk of the city/town where drawn; only qualifying nonprofits **in existence 2+ years**; state tax on gross proceeds. **Do not run a raffle.** |
| **MA ch. 93A** | A misleading statement about where the money goes is a deceptive act — AG enforcement plus a private right of action with attorney's fees. This is the statute that makes §3.2 matter. |
| **Pennsylvania** (10 P.S. §162.1 et seq., BCCO) | Governs soliciting *in PA*. A public school district is a government instrumentality; accredited educational institutions and their related foundations have exemptions (§162.6), and small organizations under the gross-contribution threshold with no paid solicitor are exempt from registration. **The charity confirms its own status with BCCO — you don't assume it.** |
| **Online** | A donation page is reachable from every state. Under the NASCO "Charleston Principles" framework, a charity registers where it actively targets donors. One more reason the page belongs to the charity, not to you. |

### 3.2 What you may and may not say from that stage

This is the part that actually gets people in trouble. Every line in §6 was written against this table.

**Say:**
- The charity's **full legal name** and that it is the recipient.
- What the money buys, **in units** — airframes, batteries, props, a case.
- **Control language:** "gifts go to [charity]; [charity] decides how they're spent." Never let it sound like you direct the spending.
- Your **conflict disclosure**, unprompted (§3.5).
- What happens **if the goal is exceeded.**
- On deductibility: *"[charity] will send a receipt; ask your own accountant"* — never a flat tax promise.

**Don't say:**
- **"100% of proceeds" / "every dollar."** Card processing takes 2.2–3%. Only usable if Drone Edge genuinely covers fees — and then say exactly that.
- **"Tax-deductible"** as a bare claim, and never at all if money touches a for-profit.
- A **named teacher** as the beneficiary. Earmarking to an individual destroys deductibility and looks like a personal gift; restrict to *"the UAS program at [school]."*
- Anything implying the **district endorses Drone Edge products.**
- Any number you can't document — student counts, pass rates, outcomes.
- A **guarantee** about what the school will buy, or a delivery date.
- Student names, faces, or footage without releases (§3.6).

### 3.3 Tax and receipting (charity's job, your problem if it's wrong)

- **§170(f)(8)**: gifts ≥ $250 need a contemporaneous written acknowledgment.
- **§6115 quid pro quo**: if any payment over $75 includes goods or services (a meal, a tee, a course seat), the charity must give a good-faith estimate of that value and state that only the excess is deductible. **Easiest fix: don't bundle anything with the ask.** Merch is a giveaway or it's a separate taxable retail sale.
- **MA sales tax**: selling tees at the event is a taxable sale requiring MA registration and 6.25% tax — a "suggested donation" for goods does not reliably escape this. Give merch away; keep cash on the charity's page. See [`merch.md`](merch.md).
- **Restricted-gift drafting**: write the purpose **broad enough to spend** — *"UAS program equipment and student activities"*, not *"four DJI Neos."* Add an overage clause. Restricted gifts are enforceable as charitable trusts; the AG can enforce donor intent.
- Drone Edge's own matching gift is an ordinary corporate charitable contribution, deductible if to a §170(c) organization; in an LLC/S-corp it flows to the personal return.

### 3.4 Grant compliance — read both award letters before anything is public

Pull Grant #1 (autonomous flight) and Grant #2 (terrain/runoff) and check each for:

1. **Publicity / acknowledgment clause** — many funders require written approval before press, naming, or logo use. Presenting funded work on a public stage can trip this.
2. **Restrictions on using the funded project for fundraising or commercial promotion.**
3. **Grantee of record** — district or Drone Edge? If the district, the project is theirs; you need written permission to present it publicly *and* to fundraise on its behalf.
4. **Equipment title and disposition.**
5. **Matching requirements** — a fundraiser might count as match, or might violate a non-supplanting term.
6. **Federal pass-through** (Perkins, Title, ESSER, NSF, etc.): under **2 CFR 200.442 fundraising costs are unallowable** — you cannot charge any cost of this event to the grant. Equipment bought with federal funds carries title and disposition conditions (**2 CFR 200.313**), and procurement must follow the district's written conflict-of-interest standards (**2 CFR 200.318(c)**).

### 3.5 The conflict-of-interest trap — the biggest reputational risk here

Drone Edge sells drone curriculum to schools (A5). If Drone Edge raises money for a district and that district then spends it on Drone Edge products, the fundraiser becomes a sales channel funded by donors. Under a federal-funds procurement lens it can taint the purchase; without federal funds it's still the story that ends the relationship.

**Fix — commit to all three, out loud:**
1. Funds buy **third-party hardware only**. No Drone Edge curriculum, licenses, or consulting, ever, from this money.
2. **The district procures**, on its own paperwork, from whatever vendor it chooses.
3. **Disclose the vendor relationship from the stage before the ask**, in one sentence. Volunteering it costs you nothing; having it discovered costs you everything.

Also: PA's Public Official and Employee Ethics Act (65 Pa.C.S. §1103) and district gift policies constrain what staff can accept. Donations go to the **school board** for acceptance under its gifts/grants/donations policy — not to a teacher, not to a classroom, not to a person.

### 3.6 School side

- **Board acceptance**: get the district's gift-acceptance path in writing before you promise anything on stage. Board agendas run on a monthly cycle — check whether an October or November meeting is needed.
- **FERPA / media** (20 U.S.C. §1232g; 34 CFR 99): student photos and video can be PII depending on context. Get **signed media releases** — parent/guardian for minors — covering promotional *and fundraising* use, any medium. Honor directory-information opt-outs. Never pair a name with a face without a release.
- **Privacy of the runoff imagery**: terrain models of neighborhoods capture private property. Don't publish identifiable addresses.
- **No students in Boston** (A6, 18+ event). If that ever changes: field-trip approval, chaperones, parental consent, insurance.

### 3.7 Event side

- **Organizer permission, in writing.** Soliciting at someone else's event without it is the fastest way to get cut off mid-sentence. Note that a money ask right before the awards **competes with the organizers' own sponsors** — disclose the ask when you negotiate the slot (§4), never spring it.
- **Better slot than the awards**: ask for time *before* awards, or at Saturday dinner. The stage ask is awareness; the actual conversion happens at your table and in the follow-up email.
- **Venue**: private venue rules + a certificate of insurance are likely required.
- **Insurance**: event liability, plus non-owned aircraft liability if anything flies.

### 3.8 If anything flies at the ask

Any flight supporting the business is a **Part 107** operation: certificated RPIC, registered aircraft, and **§107.39** limits on flight over people (a sub-250 g Tello-class aircraft may fit Category 1, but verify — don't assume). Outdoors anywhere near Boston means controlled airspace and **LAANC** authorization. Indoors is the simple answer. Venue and insurer sign off either way.

### 3.9 Ranked — what gets you in trouble fastest

1. Drone Edge collecting the money (Path B) → MA co-venturer/solicitor exposure and the vendor story.
2. Saying "100% of proceeds" or "tax-deductible" loosely → ch. 93A.
3. Fundraising on a stage without the organizer's written OK.
4. Money coming back to Drone Edge, or appearing to.
5. Showing a student's face without a release.
6. Presenting grant-funded work in violation of a funder's publicity clause.
7. A raffle. Just don't.

## 5. Story architecture

Three threads (autonomous flight grant, terrain/runoff grant, a Boston hackathon) and a fundraiser. They only cohere around one sentence:

> **A drone that flies itself is only interesting once you point it at something that matters.**

| Thread | Role in the story |
|---|---|
| **Grant #1 — autonomous flight** | The **how**. The machine decides. It's also your legitimate reason to be in that room: this weekend is an autonomy classroom. |
| **The hackathon (C-UAS)** | The **proving ground**. Detect → track → defeat, against an adversary that fights back. The room's own weekend. |
| **Grant #2 — terrain modeling / runoff** | The **why**. Identical stack — perceive, decide, act — aimed at the ground. Output is a map that tells a township where its water goes. Civic, local, legible to anyone. |
| **The gap** | **Two drones for a class that needs four.** One small, concrete, fixable number. That is the ask. |

Why this works on that specific audience: they just spent 48 hours learning that the hard part isn't the model, it's the hardware being in the room and working. "We are two airframes short" is a problem they felt personally on Sunday afternoon with ten shared Tellos.

**Arc:** the room's weekend → same skill, different target → who actually does it (public high school, not a lab) → the gap in numbers → the ask, disclosed and clean → close on the students, not on yourself.

**Length:** 5 minutes, hard. The awards run 17:30 and the event ends 18:00 — nobody's attention survives longer, and the organizers need their own moment.

## 6. Keynote script

### 6.1 Five-minute version

> Two days ago somebody in this room wrote a reward function that convinced a drone the smartest possible move was to hover in a corner and never leave.
>
> By this afternoon, some of you had something that moved on purpose, at a thing that was moving on purpose back. That's the whole job. Perceive, decide, act — on a deadline, on bad Wi-Fi, on ten minutes of battery.
>
> I want to take that exact sentence and point it at the ground.
>
> [DISTANCE] miles south of here there's a creek behind a high school. When it rains hard, the parking lot sends its water somewhere — and until somebody actually surveys it, nobody knows where. A crew with a total station costs real money and takes a week. A drone flying a grid it planned itself gives you a centimeter-scale terrain model in forty minutes, and from that model you can compute where every drop goes. Which basement floods. Which bank erodes. Which parking lot is quietly dumping into a creek.
>
> Same stack you built this weekend. Perception, a planner, a controller. Different payload, and a different adversary — this one is water, and water wins on a long enough timeline.
>
> The people doing that work are high school students in Delaware County, Pennsylvania. Not a lab. Not grad students. Juniors and seniors in a public school, with a teacher who said yes.
>
> We got two grants this year. One is autonomous flight — which is the honest reason I'm here, because this weekend is the best autonomy classroom in the Northeast and it cost me a tank of gas. The other is terrain modeling and runoff analysis, which is the part where a seventeen-year-old hands a township a map that changes a decision.
>
> Here's the unglamorous part. The grant bought **two** drones. The class has [N] students. The teacher asked for **four**.
>
> That's it. That's the bottleneck. Not compute. Not a model. Two airframes.
>
> So I'm going to do the thing nobody likes at the end of a hackathon. I'll do it in ninety seconds and then get out of the way.
>
> There's a QR code on this slide. It goes to **[CHARITY LEGAL NAME]** — that's [the school district / its education foundation]. It does **not** go to my company. And you should know before you decide anything: I sell drone curriculum to schools for a living. So let me say this in front of all of you, on the record.
>
> Not one dollar of this comes back to Drone Edge. No curriculum, no licenses, no consulting. It buys airframes, batteries, and props — and **[the district] buys them**, on its own paperwork, from whichever vendor it wants. They decide. Not me.
>
> [AMOUNT] puts one more drone in that room. [AMOUNT] finishes the set of four and pays for the batteries that make a class period actually work. Anything past that stays with the same program — the teacher has a list, and it is not a short list.
>
> I'll match the first [AMOUNT] myself, tonight.
>
> [CHARITY] will send you a receipt. Ask your own accountant what's deductible — I'm not going to promise you a tax outcome from a stage.
>
> And if you'd rather give hardware than money: we will take airframes. Talk to me at the table.
>
> One last thing, and it's the real reason I asked for these five minutes.
>
> Teams of four. That was the rule this weekend. Somewhere in the next few years, one of those four seats belongs to a kid who learned to fly in a public high school classroom — because the equipment happened to be in the room on the day she got curious. That's the entire mechanism. The equipment has to be there on the day they get curious.
>
> You spent two days building machines that decide things. Decide this one.
>
> [CHARITY LEGAL NAME], QR code, ninety seconds. Now let's give these people their prizes.
